# Checklists by step

Every line gets a «yes» or a concrete fix. Report line by line, never with the single word «checked».

## Step 1. Critical reading of the parameter list

- Parameter or calculation? If it is derived from other rows, remove it. It comes back as a row of the model.
- One unit, and it is a unit. «Currency per month» or «per cent», not «units per day = ovens × batches».
- No duplicates. Two values describing the same thing cannot both be mandatory.
- Taxes and financing are present. Regime, rate, payroll contributions, the source of the start-up money.
- One-off start-up costs are present. Fit-out, deposit, signage, point of sale, permits.
- Technical settings are present. Start date, horizon, ramp-up period.
- Every row has a source. That is the route map for steps 2 and 3: what to ask the client, what to research.
- Proportionality. A single location does not need demand elasticity or assortment matrices. Language models tend to overcomplicate.

## Step 1. The parameters file

- One table, headers in the first row, a filter on the headers.
- Units in their own column, no formulas and no lists of options.
- The «Required for the base model» column is filled for every row.
- Text wrapping on, column widths to fit the content.

## Step 2. Before sending the quote

- The price sits next to the scope. The client sees what they pay for: stages and hours, not a single figure.
- The result is described as things. A model file, a note, a deck, a meeting. Not «analysis and consulting».
- The deadlines are real. Counted from receiving the client's data, not from signing.
- What you need from the client, with dates.
- What is not included. Bookkeeping, a bank-ready business plan, support after delivery. One line.
- The figures match the passport. Horizon, format and region are the ones you discussed.
- Nothing extra promised. Language models like to add a «guaranteed result». Remove it.
- The client's name typed by hand, into the file that goes to the client.

## Step 3. Before the model

- Every market figure has a link and a date. The source opens and carries that exact figure.
- The three decisive figures opened personally: the main revenue driver, the share of direct costs, the tax rate.
- Legislation from the primary source, with the article or section number.
- Figures fit our format and city. Not a chain, not the capital, not a different segment.
- Units match the parameters. Per cent of revenue, currency per month, days.
- The unit cost calculation is in units of currency, not thousands. Conversions happen inside formulas.
- The Scenarios sheet is filled. Three values for every key parameter.
- Every value found has been sent to the client. The model is calculated after their reply, or carries the note «awaiting confirmation».

## Step 4. The model, looked at with your own eyes

- References, not numbers. Click five random calculation cells: each holds a formula pointing at the parameter sheet.
- No stray zeros. Payroll, rent, utilities and investment are not zero in any working month.
- Investment is in place. Fit-out, equipment, deposit and opening stock sit in investing activities in their own months.
- The cash flow statement reconciles. Closing cash equals opening cash plus the flow, and the first month opens at zero.
- Payback is calculated. There is a payback month, or the cell is red because it never arrives.
- The switches work. Change the scenario and the model recalculates. Set the horizon to 24 instead of 36 and nothing breaks.
- Tax follows the regime from the passport. The minimum tax, if the jurisdiction has one, is included, and so are mandatory owner contributions.
- Explanations are in place. Every row carries a «How it is calculated» column a non-specialist can read.

## Step 5. Before the next step

- The comparison is live. Change a parameter on the parameter sheet and all three columns recalculate.
- The base column matches the figures of the selected scenario above.
- The zero row of the sensitivity tables equals the base scenario on all three measures.
- The ranking makes sense. The top places belong to parameters that multiply revenue.
- The break point lies between the pessimistic and the base scenario.
- Financing is resolved. It is clear in which scenario the money is enough and in which it is not.
- The conclusions carry figures. Every sentence can be traced to the file, with no «good» and no «risky».
- Nothing by hand. Not one number pasted from a run.
- The calculation mode is verified: data tables recalculate instead of showing a stale cache.

## Step 6. Before sending to the client

- The reconciliation passed. Every point matches the spreadsheet to the last unit of currency, and the table is in the folder.
- Three figures verified by hand: investment need, payback and base scenario profit on the page equal the Scenarios sheet.
- The sliders respond predictably. Main driver up: profit up, payback earlier. Fixed costs up: the other way round.
- Reset works. After any movement the button restores the base scenario.
- The note and the screens are live. Move a parameter and the figures in the note and on the screens change.
- Print is readable. The note prints dark on white, not pale grey.
- It opens on a phone. Send the file to yourself in a messenger and open it: everything legible, sliders usable with a thumb.
- It works offline. Turn off the network, the page still runs.
- No client data. No company name, no personal names, only the niche and the city.

## Step 7. Before handover

- Post-meeting edits applied to the spreadsheet and the page, reconciliation repeated.
- The note downloaded from the page with the final figures, the client's name typed in by hand.
- Estimates are flagged. The client can see which figures still await their confirmation.
- No advice to go ahead. Conditions and risks only.
- The contents match the quote. Everything promised in the chosen package is delivered.
- The page opens on the client's device. Sent in advance and confirmed.
- The acceptance document is signed.
- The project folder is complete. Every version and source file is in place.
