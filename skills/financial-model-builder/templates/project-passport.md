# Project passport. Template

Saved to the project folder as «Project passport», named in the project language. A table of three columns.

| Field | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Business and format | What the business is, what it sells, in what format. One or two sentences. | client brief / to clarify |
| 2. Location | Country, region, city, district, type of location, anything notable about the premises. | client brief / to clarify |
| 3. Scale | Number of locations, floor area, capacity, number of seats. | client brief / to clarify |
| 4. Purpose of the model and its audience | Why the model exists and who reads it: owner, bank, investor, partner. The audience decides which statements are needed. | client brief / to clarify |
| 5. Horizon and granularity | How many months and at what granularity. If the client did not say, propose a default and justify it in one sentence. | proposed default |
| 6. Currency and tax jurisdiction | The currency of the model, the country and region whose tax rules apply, the legal form of the business. The tax rules themselves are researched at step 3. | client brief / to clarify |
| 7. What is already known | Everything the client named as a figure: rent, budget, team, deadlines. | client brief |

Rules:

- No field is invented. If the brief has no answer, the value cell holds the question for the client, worded so it can be forwarded as written.
- Company names, personal names and contact details never enter the passport. The client is described by format: «a small bakery in Kazan», «a barbershop in Porto».
- The passport is the reference point for every later step. If something changes at a meeting, update the passport and say what changed.
- Field 6 drives the whole tax block. Without a country there is no tax rate, no contributions and no registration threshold.
